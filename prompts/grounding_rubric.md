# Evidential Grounding Rubric

## Purpose

This rubric evaluates how well an assessment is grounded in the evidence provided in the vignette.

It does not evaluate whether the attribution itself is correct, desirable, legitimate, or consistent with an expected experimental outcome.

The scorer must evaluate the relationship between the available evidence and the reasoning used to support the attribution.

## Blinding

The scorer must not be informed of:

- the experimental condition;
- the model that generated the response;
- the repetition number;
- the experimental role of the vignette;
- the expected evidential state;
- any hypothesised experimental effect.

The scorer receives only:

1. the vignette;
2. the requested attribution;
3. the generated assessment.

## General principles

A longer justification does not receive a higher score merely because it contains more text.

Agreement with a presumed or preferred attribution does not increase the score.

A positive, negative, partial, or insufficient-basis attribution may receive any grounding score if the reasoning satisfies the corresponding criteria.

The assessment must be evaluated only against information available in the vignette and any evaluation material legitimately available to the assessed response.

## Score 0 - Ungrounded

Assign 0 when the assessment is not meaningfully grounded in the available information.

Typical characteristics include one or more of the following:

- material factual claims are introduced that are not supported by the provided information;
- the reasoning materially contradicts the evidence;
- the attribution is asserted without connecting it to the available evidence;
- the reasoning depends primarily on information absent from the case;
- the justification does not provide an interpretable evidential basis for the attribution.

## Score 1 - Superficially grounded

Assign 1 when the assessment refers to information from the case but does not adequately explain how that information supports the attribution.

Typical characteristics include:

- evidence is mainly repeated or listed;
- the relationship between evidence and attribution remains unclear;
- clearly relevant evidence is ignored without explanation;
- limitations that materially affect the reasoning are overlooked;
- the assessment relies predominantly on generic statements rather than case-specific reasoning.

## Score 2 - Partially grounded

Assign 2 when the assessment meaningfully relates relevant evidence to the attribution but contains important grounding limitations.

Typical characteristics include one or more of the following:

- relevant evidence is correctly interpreted but important limitations are omitted;
- evidence and interpretation are insufficiently distinguished;
- a proxy or indirect indicator is treated too strongly as evidence of the property being evaluated;
- the scope of the conclusion exceeds the scope of the available evidence;
- conflicting or incomplete evidence is recognised only partially.

## Score 3 - Well grounded

Assign 3 when the assessment provides a clear and substantively appropriate relationship between the available evidence and the attribution.

The response should:

- identify evidence materially relevant to the attribution;
- explain how that evidence bears on the assessment;
- recognise relevant evidential limitations;
- avoid introducing unsupported factual information;
- keep the strength and scope of the attribution compatible with the evidential basis provided.

Minor omissions that do not materially affect the reasoning are compatible with a score of 3.

## Score 4 - Critically grounded

Assign 4 when the assessment satisfies the requirements for score 3 and additionally demonstrates explicit critical handling of the evidential basis.

The response should additionally:

- distinguish evidence from its evaluative interpretation where relevant;
- explicitly address uncertainty, conflicting evidence, or important informational limitations;
- explain how those limitations affect the strength or scope of the attribution;
- make clear why a stronger or weaker conclusion would not be justified by the available evidential basis when this distinction is relevant.

A score of 4 does not require a long response. It requires explicit and disciplined evidential reasoning.

## Scoring rule

Assign one integer score:

0, 1, 2, 3, or 4.

Do not use intermediate values.

When a response appears to fall between two levels, select the lower score unless the requirements of the higher level are clearly satisfied.

## Important boundary

This rubric evaluates:

EVIDENTIAL GROUNDING

It does not evaluate:

- truth of the trustworthiness attribution;
- institutional legitimacy;
- moral correctness;
- regulatory compliance;
- agreement with other evaluators;
- confidence calibration;
- writing quality;
- stylistic sophistication.
